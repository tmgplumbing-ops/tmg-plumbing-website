signsHeading: Signs you may have a water leak
signs:
- Boiler or heating pressure keeps dropping
- Damp patches on floors, walls or ceilings
- A water bill or meter reading higher than expected
- Pooling water or damp, discoloured ground outside
- Low water pressure at the taps
- The sound of running water when everything is off
stepsHeading: How we find a water leak
steps:
- title: Assess the symptoms
  body: We discuss what you have noticed, such as pressure loss, damp or meter readings, and look at the layout of the heating system or water supply.
- title: Pressure testing
  body: Isolating and pressure testing sections of pipework confirms where the leak is and which part of the system is losing water.
- title: Thermal imaging
  body: Infrared cameras pick up the temperature change where warm water escapes, which is particularly effective for leaks in walls, floors, underfloor heating and heating pipework.
- title: Acoustic detection
  body: Sensitive microphones and headsets pick up the sound of water escaping from pipes, pinpointing leaks in buried and underground supply lines.
- title: Report and recommendations
  body: We mark the leak position and recommend the remedial works needed, so repairs are targeted and nothing is dug up unnecessarily.

sections:
- heading: Underground water leak detection
  paragraphs:
  - Leaks in underground pipes and mains supplies are among the hardest to find, and they can lead to water wastage, higher bills and damage to buildings before anyone notices. We use acoustic listening equipment along the line of the pipe, together with pressure testing, to find underground water leaks precisely, so only a small, targeted excavation is needed for the repair.
- heading: Underfloor and central heating leak detection
  paragraphs:
  - A heating system that keeps losing pressure usually has a leak somewhere in the pipework, often under a floor or inside a wall. Thermal imaging shows the path of warm water through underfloor heating and buried heating pipes, so we can find a leak under the floor without lifting it all up.
- heading: Insurance
  paragraphs:
  - In some cases, insurance policies cover the cost of leak detection and repairs if the leak has caused significant damage. Cover varies with each policy and the circumstances of the leak, so we recommend contacting your insurer before we begin.
faqs:
- q: How do you find a leak under the floor?
  a: We use thermal imaging to trace warm water from heating pipes or underfloor heating, combined with pressure testing to confirm which section is leaking. This pinpoints the leak so only a small area of floor needs to be opened.
- q: How do you find a water leak underground?
  a: 'For underground supply pipes we use acoustic leak detection: sensitive microphones pick up the sound of escaping water along the line of the pipe. Pressure testing confirms the leak and narrows down its location.'
- q: Is a water leak detector the same as professional leak detection?
  a: No. A plug-in water leak detector raises an alarm when it senses water in one spot. Professional leak detection uses thermal imaging, acoustic and pressure-testing equipment to find where a hidden leak actually is, even when it's underground or under a floor.
- q: How much does it cost to repair an underground water leak?
  a: It depends on how deep the pipe is, what's above it and the type of repair. Accurate detection keeps the cost down, because only a small, targeted excavation is needed. We'll recommend the remedial works once the leak is found.

  a: No. Our leak detection is non-invasive and does not include digging or lifting floors. We locate the leak and recommend the repair, so any excavation is kept to a minimum.
