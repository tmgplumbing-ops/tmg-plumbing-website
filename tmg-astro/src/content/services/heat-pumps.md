about:
  heading: How an air source heat pump works
  paragraphs:
  - A heat pump works on the same principle as a fridge, in reverse. An outdoor unit takes heat from the air around your property and raises it to a more useful temperature. That heat is passed into your central heating system, and into a hot water cylinder if you want it to heat your hot water too.
  - Because it runs on electricity rather than burning fuel, a heat pump gives off no combustion emissions at the house. It works best at lower water temperatures, which is why it pairs so well with underfloor heating and a well-insulated home.

sections:
- heading: Heat pumps and underfloor heating
  paragraphs:
  - A heat pump runs most efficiently at lower water temperatures, which is exactly how underfloor heating operates. If you are building or renovating, the two make a natural pair. A heat pump can also run a radiator system, but the radiators need to suit the lower temperatures, so it is worth discussing at the site visit.
- heading: Keep the system clean
  paragraphs:
  - A heat pump circulates water through pipework just like any other heating system, so the quality of that water still matters. If you are connecting a heat pump to an older system, it is worth having the pipework cleaned first. We can power flush the system and treat the water, so the new equipment starts with a clean circuit.

faqs:
- q: How does a heat pump work?
  a: It takes heat from the outside air and raises it to a useful temperature using a refrigerant and compressor, then passes that heat into your heating system and, if you want, a hot water cylinder.
- q: Does my home need to be well insulated?
  a: Yes. A heat pump works efficiently in a well-insulated home with good windows and doors. At the site visit we look at your home and tell you honestly whether it suits a heat pump.
- q: Does a heat pump only work with underfloor heating?
  a: No, but it works most efficiently with it, because both run at lower temperatures than a traditional radiator system. A heat pump can also run radiators, depending on the design of the system.
- q: Can a heat pump heat my hot water?
  a: Yes. It can be connected to a hot water cylinder to heat your water as well as your heating.
- q: What types of heat pump are there?
  a: Air source, ground source and water source. Air source is the most common, and it is the type we specialise in.
- q: Does the outdoor unit have to be on an outside wall?
  a: The outdoor unit of an air source heat pump has to be outside. The air handler part can be installed inside your home.
- q: Are there grants for heat pumps?
  a: Grants may be available through SEAI for eligible homes. Check the SEAI website for what applies at the time you are planning your project.
- q: Do heat pumps need servicing?
  a: Yes. Like any heating system, a heat pump benefits from regular checks. If it seems to run constantly in summer, it may need to be looked at by the installer or a registered service engineer.
