stepsHeading: How we power flush heating systems in Tipperary
steps:
- title: Inspection and water analysis
  body: We check the system and test a sample of the heating water for corrosion and pH, so we know its condition before we start.
- title: Filtration
  body: Our flushing unit filters sludge, metal particles and debris out of the radiators, pipework and underfloor heating. Usually there is no need to drain the system or use chemicals.
- title: High-velocity, low-pressure flushing
  body: Clean water is circulated at high velocity but low pressure, clearing each radiator and circuit without straining older pipework.
- title: Magnetic filtration
  body: Magnetic filters capture the metal oxide particles that cause pinhole leaks and damage boilers.
- title: Demineralisation
  body: The water is passed through a demineralising unit so it is non-corrosive, slowing future build-up.
- title: Final check
  body: We confirm every radiator heats evenly and the boiler or heat pump is running efficiently.

faqs:
- q: How much does a power flush cost in Tipperary?
  a: The price depends mainly on how many radiators you have and the condition of the system. We confirm a fixed price before any work begins.
- q: Should I power flush before fitting a heat pump?
  a: Yes. A heat pump runs at lower temperatures and is sensitive to poor water quality, so cleaning and demineralising the system first helps it reach its efficiency and protects its warranty.
- q: How often should I power flush my heating system?
  a: We recommend every five to six years, or sooner if you notice cold spots on radiators or rising energy bills.
- q: What is the difference between power flushing and chemical flushing?
  a: Power flushing uses high velocity at low pressure, with filters and magnets, to physically remove sludge. Chemical flushing relies on cleaning agents. Power flushing is more thorough and effective for heavily clogged systems.
- q: Is power flushing safe for my heating system?
  a: Yes, when it is carried out professionally. We inspect the system first and flush at low pressure, and we will point out any concerns about old or corroded radiators before we start.
